## Anti-pattern verdict

The landing is a borderline pass. It avoids generic SaaS, agency, hospital, clinic, dashboard, and AI-tool styling. The warm mineral accent, honest proof boundary, and Threshold device provide a point of view. The remaining risk is generic “premium minimalism”: repeated small labels, large grotesk headings, ruled lists, broad whitespace, and similar two-column compositions.

## Priority issues

### P0 — Local-only form disclosure arrives after user effort

The task intentionally forbids a production backend, but users only learn that data was not sent after completing the form. Disclose local/mock behavior before the first field, make the final action a validation action rather than a fake submission, and preserve all entered values.

### P1 — Desktop proof artifact collides at 1440px

The proposal heading overlaps the services column. Stack the proposal intro above services/contact until the inner pane has sufficient width, reduce only preview typography as needed, and keep the two states credible and aligned.

### P1 — Fictional disclosure follows demo controls in reading order

The demo includes apparently actionable clinic links before the fictional notice. Move the exact fictional notice into the comparison header and render clinic controls as non-actionable specimen content.

### P1 — Mobile proof begins too late

At 390×844, the first viewport ends before the comparison begins. Keep the commercial hierarchy, then place reassurance after the proof on mobile and tighten only the opening rhythm so the comparison is visibly imminent.

### P2 — Active navigation lags after CTA navigation

Set the request navigation state synchronously when the CTA is activated, then let the section observer maintain it.

### P2 — Required form is visually unchunked

All required fields are in scope and must remain. Group them semantically into user/contact, clinic/site, and consent sections without adding cards or a multi-step workflow.
